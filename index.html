Fair value is about 3% below the market price, and 74% of 500 simulated outcomes land lower, so the call is Hold. It rests on a free-cash-flow DCF, a Monte Carlo range and a real-option value for the peptide CDMO expansion.

Why Hold: margins and the business are improving, but most of that already shows up in the price. Fair value sits just under market.

  • Upgrade if Gagillapur clears its FDA re-inspection, peptides scale faster than guided, or the stock falls toward the low end of the range.
  • Downgrade if the US generic-drug tariff exclusion narrows, a new regulatory issue appears, or the 72% North America concentration starts to hurt.

Test the valuation yourself

Move the sliders to see fair value per share. The model values the terminal year with an exit multiple, so that is the default. Switch to perpetual growth to cross-check it.

What I'd do differently. Terminal value is 86% of enterprise value, and the 12x exit multiple implies about 5.6% perpetual growth, which is high. I would lead with a perpetuity cross-check (you can try it above). My ₹828 base case also sits above the simulation median (₹708), so I would explain that gap and let operating and market drivers vary independently.

The blended target is ₹446 against a ₹581 price. It leans 80% on a cash-flow valuation because the multiples disagree widely. To get there I restated the financial statements, projected free cash flow, compared two terminal-value methods and ran five relative-valuation multiples.

The spread between methods is wide, so I weighted the cash-flow-based value most heavily rather than averaging everything.

What I'd do differently. I would explain the 80/20 weighting with a sensitivity table, and reconcile the P/E and P/B ranges with the DCF. If the methods differ by this much, I should show which assumptions cause it.

Momentum trading model: +70.7% vs the Nifty's +29.6%

A rules-based MACD and EMA200 system for Indian equities on 30-minute bars. Results come from simulated fills that include brokerage, STT and exchange fees. No real orders were placed.

150–200 hResearch and engineering
30+Script versions
12Statistical simulations
−8.9%Max drawdown
  • Win rate isn't the point. Binomial probability set a survival filter at 10 trades and a 24% win rate, removing weak stocks without cutting off the large winners.
  • Real execution. The matching engine models overnight gap-down risk, Indian broker charges, STT and exchange fees.
  • Portfolio limits. Fixed capital, no infinite margin. Time-stops and signal ranking fix allocation bias and the 17% of capital-days that sat idle.
  • Where the profit came from. Small caps earned 60% more than large caps on 66% fewer trades. Re-weighting toward small and mid caps, pharma and PSUs lifted theoretical P&L from about ₹700k to ₹1.17M.
